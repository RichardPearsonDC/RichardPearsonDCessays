Basics about Bluetooth Signals Bluetooth relies entirely on Ultra High Frequency (UHF) radio signals to function. It broadcasts over a frequency band - 2.4 GHz ISM band (2.400 to 2.4835 GHz), the same band used by Wi-Fi. It transmits at low power levels, restricting range to about 10 to 100 meters. Bluetooth signals degrade significantly even with military equipment. According to Google AI, "In a dense city targeting a ground level objective, standard military Bluetooth devices typically reach less than 30 to 100 feet (10 to 30 meters) due to severe signal degradation." Google points to three classes of Bluetooth range.  Class 1 (Industrial / Long-Range): Up to 328 feet (100 meters). These are used in industrial sensors, enterprise adapters, and specialized transmitters. Class 2 (Standard Consumer Electronics): Up to 33 feet (10 meters). Most smartphones, wireless earbuds, and portable speakers use Class 2 radios. Class 3 (Ultra Low-Power): Up to 3 feet (1 meter).
