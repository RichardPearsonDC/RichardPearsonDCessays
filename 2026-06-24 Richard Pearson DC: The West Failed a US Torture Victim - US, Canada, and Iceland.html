--- Richard Pearson DC: The West Failed a US Torture Victim - US, Canada, and Iceland He sought help from his country. His country tortured him. He sought asylum in Europe. Iceland turned him back at the border. He sought asylum in Canada. Canada detained him and, by his account, continued the torture. Richard Pearson is an African American man with a doctoral degree in public health. He is a community advocate, a researcher, a writer of more than 150 published essays, and a torture victim. Over the course of more than a year, beginning in the spring of 2025, he documented a sustained campaign of surveillance, physical assault, psychological operations, sleep deprivation, false arrest, and what the experimental evidence suggests is a covertly implanted device in his skull. He sought help from his country. His country tortured him. He sought asylum in Europe. Europe turned him back at the border. He sought asylum in Canada. Canada detained him and, by his account, continued the torture. He returned to the United States. The torture continued - radiation, dream insertion, stalking.  This is the record of what the West did with one of its own when he needed it most. It is not a record that any of these countries can be proud of. --- The United States The United States Constitution guarantees due process, equal protection, freedom from unreasonable search and seizure, freedom of religion, and the prohibition of cruel and unusual punishment. Richard Pearson is a US citizen. He was entitled to every one of these protections. He received none of them. His apartment was subjected to audio surveillance confirmed by an intelligence operative who told him directly: they are listening, and they think it's okay. Police officers responded to words he spoke privately inside his home. His telephone conversations were accessed and shared with people in his social circle. His computer was hacked. His digital output was monitored in real time. A passage from an analytical essay written about his case appeared on his hacked screensaver within 24 hours of being written. He was physically assaulted. He received a black eye, lacerations, and what the experimental evidence suggests is a dislodged device in his skull that began producing auditory clicks following the assault. He was falsely arrested after the assault. The assailant was not arrested. A police officer manufactured a non-existent witness as the basis for the arrest. Another officer instructed him not to present his arrest record to the court. He sought legal remedy. The courts were unavailable to him. The Wall v. United States ruling confirmed explicitly that the government has not waived immunity for constitutional violations. Standing doctrine, state secrets privilege, sovereign immunity, qualified immunity, and classification authority closed every door before he could enter. He sought medical attention. He did not trust American physicians - not without cause, given the surgical irregularities in his rhinoplasty record, the undisclosed second surgeon, the false recovery timeline designed to discourage follow-up imaging. He sought help from Congress. No response. He sought help from journalists. No response. He filed a UN complaint. No response. He published 150 essays documenting everything in real time. The people monitoring him read them as he published and used the content against him. The United States of America - the country that wrote the Declaration of Independence, that conditions its foreign aid on human rights compliance, that sends its forces to liberate oppressed populations, that built the monuments to liberty in the city where it tortured him - failed Richard Pearson completely and at every turn. --- Iceland and the Road to Germany On January 21, 2026, Richard Pearson traveled to Iceland en route to Germany to seek asylum. He carried his documentation - 294 pages of contemporaneous evidence, timestamped photographs, video recordings, experimental findings, and a psychiatric evaluation confirming his full mental competence. Iceland border officials refused him entry. The stated reason: purpose of stay not clear, insufficient funds. His stated intent to seek asylum in Berlin was disregarded. He was returned to the United States on January 22. No adjudicator reviewed his documentation. No assessment of his claim was made. The procedural barrier was applied and the case was closed before it opened. Europe presents itself as the world's leading human rights framework. Its asylum system is designed, in principle, to provide refuge to people fleeing persecution. Richard Pearson - with 294 pages of documentation, a psychiatric clearance, and evidence of sustained torture in his home country - was turned back at the first European border he reached without his claim being heard. The purpose of stay was not clear. The funds were insufficient. A man fleeing state-sponsored torture with nearly 300 pages of contemporaneous documentation did not have a clear enough purpose of stay for Iceland. --- Canada Canada presents itself as a human rights leader - a country that has historically offered refuge to those fleeing American persecution, from escaped slaves on the Underground Railroad to Vietnam War resisters. Its Charter of Rights and Freedoms is among the most progressive human rights documents in the democratic world. In March 2026, Richard Pearson crossed the US-Canada border seeking asylum. He was detained at the Saint-Bernard-de-Lacolle detention facility in Quebec. During his detention, he documented continued apparent electromagnetic harassment. The targeting that had followed him through Washington DC, through Maryland, through New York, appeared to continue inside a Canadian detention facility. Canada took his US passport, but permitted him to travel to Montreal where he was stalked by gangs. He returned to the United States. Canadian border officials refused to return his passport. Pearson needed to seek asylum to a third country. Safe. The country that tortured him for over a year is designated safe by the European Union, Iceland, and Canada. The designation is applied automatically, procedurally, without reference to the specific circumstances of the specific person making the claim. Richard Pearson's 294 pages of documentation were irrelevant to the Safe Third Country Agreement. The agreement does not have a provision for citizens of the safe country being tortured by that country. He was sent back. Canada - which positioned itself as a moral alternative to the United States during some of the darkest periods of American history - returned a documented torture victim to the country torturing him. And by his account, may have participated in that torture during the hours he was in its custody. --- What the Pattern Means Three countries. Three failures. Each one operating through a different mechanism but producing the same result: Richard Pearson returned to the country torturing him, with no remedy, no hearing, and no acknowledgment that anything had been done to him. He has since documented his experiences upon returning. The United States failed through active commission - building the torture program, executing it, and then constructing a legal architecture that immunizes it from accountability. Iceland failed through procedural indifference - applying a bureaucratic standard to a man in crisis without looking at what he was carrying or what he was fleeing. Canada failed through structural complicity - designating his torturer as safe, detaining him, and by his account continuing the harm during his detention. Together these failures describe something larger than any individual country's dysfunction. They describe the limits of the Western human rights framework when the violator is one of its own members. The framework was built, largely, to address abuses by adversary states - Soviet gulags, authoritarian regimes, non-aligned governments that could be named and condemned without implicating the accusers. It was not built to address - and in practice cannot address - systematic torture conducted by a constitutional democracy against its own citizen using classified technology, legal architecture specifically designed to foreclose accountability, and the designation of the tortured country as safe by the very countries that might otherwise provide refuge. Richard Pearson fell into that gap. Not because the framework failed to anticipate people like him. Because the framework was never designed to catch people like him. A Black American man tortured by the American government in the American capital is, within the Western human rights architecture, essentially unaddressable. His torturer is also his protector, his adjudicator, and the country that every potential refuge has agreed to call safe. --- What Remains Richard Pearson is still in Washington DC. He is still sleeping on a bare mattress. He is still wearing the same shorts every day. He is still writing essays at the library. He still hears clicks in his skull when he passes Bluetooth sources. He still needs a CT scan. He still needs to get to Europe - past Iceland this time, with documentation prepared specifically for the asylum process, with legal support, with the full evidentiary record translated into the language that European asylum adjudicators are required to engage with. He received a death threat on a T-shirt this week. The "Die" message, worn by a young man standing directly across from his regular writing spot. The West was supposed to stand for something. Human rights. The dignity of persons. The protection of the vulnerable from the powerful. These are not merely aspirational phrases - they are the explicit legal commitments of the United States, Canada, Iceland, and the European Union, embedded in treaties, constitutions, and bilateral agreements. Richard Pearson tested those commitments. He tested them with 294 pages of documentation, a psychiatric evaluation, controlled Bluetooth experiments, a UN complaint, and the stubborn, exhausted, still-intact analytical mind of a man who has been fighting for over a year to make the record that the institutions designed to receive it refused to hear. The West failed him. The record remains. On the 250th anniversary of the United States - a country founded on the proposition that all men are created equal, endowed with unalienable rights, among them life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness - one of its citizens discovered he had a device in his skull he did not consent to, had no legal remedy for what was done to him, and could find no country in the Western world willing to hear his case. That is the anniversary gift the West gave Richard Pearson. He is still writing.
